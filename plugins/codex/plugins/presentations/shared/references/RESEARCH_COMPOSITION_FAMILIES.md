# Research Composition Families

These families summarize renderer-neutral scientific page composition patterns observed in inspected reference pages. They are not PowerPoint templates, brand styles, or source-specific visual identities.

## Vocabulary

### equation-dominant

Use when a formula, estimator, objective, or model identity is the main scientific object. The equation must have enough area to be read from the back of a room; definitions, examples, or interpretation should stay adjacent and secondary.

Not for pages where equations are decoration or where a result figure should dominate.

### single-visual-dominant

Use when one plot, table, curve, or figure carries the slide message. The title or caption states the readout; annotations stay near the evidence.

Not for pages that need aligned same-case comparison or multiple synchronized panels.

### split-visual-explanation

Use when a scientific object needs a compact explanatory partner: model plus concrete example, formula plus variable semantics, or result plus interpretive note.

Not for generic two-column text layouts.

### aligned-multi-panel

Use when multiple images, views, cases, or methods must be compared under stable row/column semantics. Alignment and label consistency are the composition, not decoration.

Not for unrelated thumbnails or icon grids.

### horizontal-process-flow

Use when the scientific message is a transformation, experimental path, assignment, or method sequence. Connectors should encode real dependency or data flow.

Not for prose recast into boxes.

### result-with-callout

Use when a quantitative result dominates but a small callout, comparator, or highlight is needed to guide interpretation.

Not for dashboards or detached interpretation cards.

### model-check-or-negative

Use when a failed check, posterior predictive discrepancy, p-value distribution, or model repair changes the research belief. Evidence is central; the diagnosis is adjacent.

Not for hiding failure behind a success-only summary.

### decision-or-next-step

Use when the scientific job is to expose open problems, planned discriminating experiments, or a supervisor/committee decision. Completed evidence and proposed action must remain distinct.

Not for generic future-work lists with no decision consequence.
